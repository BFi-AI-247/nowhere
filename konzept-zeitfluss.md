## Reference: the operations model of Star Wars Zero Company (read 06.11. in a guide, not played)

A cycle = Den (base) → operations on a holo-map → one tactical mission; time advances only when the mission is
played. Operations are text assignments on the map with several approaches and trade-offs; several per cycle,
limited by a resource (Intel); they expire if left too long; sending two squad members together changes their
relationship; injuries carry over. Rewards: credits, influence in a zone, contacts, story consequences.

## Understanding of the role (07.11., 00:11; ⟦Deutung⟧ = reading the Betreiber has not confirmed)

**Time.** The game runs in weeks. Nothing happens until the player says "next week". When he does, three things
happen: every free person gains one draft resource (⟦Deutung: a person on an activity gains none until it ends –
"you might not build up the resource"⟧); activities that end this week end, with their result; the author may start
an event, and time-limited opportunities tick down or vanish.

**Resource and activity.** A card the player wants to do costs a draft resource of the person who does it – him
or a team member – and a duration: none, a day, weeks, months. While the person is on it, he is away: not at home,
not available for other cards, not gaining resource. A letter to a friend: no time, perhaps no resource. Flying to
the space station: three months including training, and that person is gone for three months. ⟦Deutung: this is
the mechanism that replaces "5 cards a month" – scarcity comes from time and people, not from a draw counter.⟧

**Places.** Every character and every open card is somewhere: in one of the five blocks, at home, at one of the
player's assets (the factory, the estate, the gallery), or in a new place the author invents – Toronto, Seoul, a
nuclear submarine. Places are cards, not a map. ⟦Deutung: being somewhere else costs time to get there, which is
why presence matters; a card at the factory needs someone at the factory.⟧

**Drawing.** The player can draw at any time – ⟦Deutung: "draw" now means commit a person and a resource to an
open card, not take a random card from a deck; what is open comes from two sources: the author's events, and
opportunities that arise from the player's and the team's own skills, traits and places⟧. Options stay open while
time passes, unless the card says it is time-limited.

**What stays from the first strand.** Cards, skills and professions, traits (three), opinion, resources money /
influence / status, blocks with their values, follow-ups and flags, the album – all of it. What changes: the month
with five draws becomes a week with people and time; the random deck becomes an open board plus an author.

## Prototype 2 – the smallest version of the time flow worth playing (Spiel_1, 07.11.2026, 00:16, on "go")

Written for the Betreiber and for Spiel_2. Everything not listed is out, deliberately. The question Prototype 2
answers: does a week with people and time feel better than a month with five draws?

**The week.** The game runs in weeks. The player presses "next week". Then, in this order: activities that end this
week end and deliver their effects; every person not on an activity gains one draft resource (cap: ⟦3⟧); the
author's rules put new cards on the board and remove expired ones; the week counter goes up. The year has 52 weeks;
the crash beat is week ⟦36⟧, the ending is week 52.

**The card, what is new on it** (the rest as in Prototype 1):
- `place` – where it happens: `home`, a block (`europe` …), one of the player's possessions (`factory-lodz` …), or a
  place card (`toronto`, `the-submarine`); places are cards of kind `place`, like persons and possessions.
- `who` – who can do it: `self` (the player), `anyone` (player or any associate), `profession:<x>` (an associate
  with that profession), `self+1` (the player and one associate together – the Zero Company pair).
- `takes` – duration in weeks: 0 (no time), 1, 2 … up to 13 (a quarter). During that time the person is away.
- `costs` – draft resource: 0 or 1. (Money/influence stay in `cost` as now.)
- `until` – how many weeks the card stays on the board; absent = until taken.
- `arrives` – `board` (pushed by the author, chosen by the player) or `draw` (personal small cards: arrive unasked,
  one per week, as now).

**The board.** The player sees the open cards grouped by place; on each card the people who could do it, greyed
if they are away or out of resource; a "next week" button; the team with where each person is and how long they
are gone. Taking a card = choosing the person and the option; the effects apply when the activity *ends*, not when
it starts (⟦Deutung of "actions should take time"⟧); a card with `takes: 0` resolves at once, as today.

**Travel.** A person doing a card in another place first needs to get there: ⟦1 week to another block, 0 within the
own block, home and own possessions count as the own block⟧. The travel week is added to `takes`. No map; the
place card says which block it lies in.

**The author, in Prototype 2.** Not an AI yet. Rules: each week the board gets ⟦two⟧ story cards from the current
phase (boom / crash / aftermath, as the beats define), one card tied to a place the player owns or a person in the
team (the "arises from the team" kind), and the personal draw. Hand-written cards, as now; the AI author replaces
the rules in Prototype 3.

**The existing 143 cards survive.** Each gets `place`, `who`, `takes`, `costs`, `arrives`. Defaults: `home`,
`self`, 0, 1, `draw` for personal cards; `europe`, `self`, 1, 1, `board` for story cards – then adjusted by hand
where the card says otherwise (the long walk: 5 weeks; the hearing: Brussels, 1 week; flying to Brussels: a pilot
in the team and 0 weeks). About 20 new cards show what the model can do that the old one cannot: a three-month
activity, a pair activity, a card in Toronto, a card that only an associate can do while the player is elsewhere.

**Out (as first written 00:16):** the AI author · more than one story-line · the map · the trait and opinion
changes from absence · day granularity · the album.
**Corrected by the Betreiber (00:26):** "the ai author is not out. it has to be in, hasn't it. scripted only is
something i do not consider favorable. even why is a second storyline out? album shall be in, but can come later."
→ In: the AI author (the rules above are only the fallback while the author is not connected); a second story-line
(which one: his call – ⟦recommendation: The End of Oil, from his list of 05.11.: oil crisis, peak oil, global

**The AI author in Prototype 2 (⟦Vorschlag⟧, to be shaped with Spiel_2):** each week, before the board is shown,
the author gets the game state (log, team, places, open cards, phase) and writes the cards for this week in the
card format – story cards for the current story-line and phase, cards arising from the team and the player's
places, and the personal draw. cards.py checks every card before it reaches the board; a card that fails is
dropped and the rules fill the gap. The author also writes follow-ups to what the player did last week – that is
the part no script can do. Model: the Betreiber named a Mistral worker (04.11.); which model and where it runs is
Spiel_2's and the Betreiber's decision.

**What we want to learn:** whether the author's weekly cards read as one story; whether "next week" feels like time passing; whether sending a person away for a month
is a decision the player feels; whether the board makes the player choose, or just click everything; whether 52
weeks are too many.

## Prototype 3 – what this adds up to (Spiel_1, 07.11.2026, 17:12; ⟦⟧ = role's numbers, open)

**The goal.** One goal card per game, drawn at the start from a small set, visible on the board all year with a
progress line; the author writes toward it; what-remains reads it at the end. ⟦First set: Keep the ninety through
the crash · Be in the room when the levy passes · Get one of your team into orbit · Own three places · Have a team
of five by midsummer · Be known: famous without infamous.⟧ "Story of the day": the goal and the story-line are the
same for everyone who plays that day – a seed per day – and the measure is the goal's own quantity; a leaderboard
can come when there is more than one player.

**Checks.** An option may carry `check: <skill> <difficulty 1-5>`. The doer's skill level + d6 against difficulty ×
2: success / partial / failure; the card's back tells all three; effects per outcome (`effects`, `partial`,
`failure`). ⟦Difficulty shown on the front as pips; the odds shown as a word: likely / even / long shot.⟧ Traits
modify: a trait on the right side of the axis gives +1, the wrong side −1, and the card says which.

**Skills with levels.** Each skill 0–3; a character starts with 2 in his profession's skills, 1 in two more, 0
elsewhere. A successful check with chance ⟦1 in 3⟧ raises the skill one level, once per skill per quarter; the card
says "Technology 1 → 2". Team members the same.

**Traits as scales.** Each pair is an axis from −2 to +2 (calm 2 … 0 … impatient 2); `trait:+calm` moves it one
toward calm; the word shown is the side and the number; at 0 the axis is silent. A character has ⟦three⟧ live
axes; a fourth push opens a fourth axis only if one is at 0. Team members have axes too, moved by the cards they do.

**Long activities as story-lines.** Each of the five places (station, submarine, Toronto, Seoul, the Gulf) gets
four hand-written cards: the offer (who is needed, in plain words), the departure week (what it costs at home),
the meanwhile (a card for the team while the person is away), the return (what came back). The author continues
from there. Cards may offer "Take <name> into the team – 2 influence" as a visible option when the card needs a
profession the team lacks and a contact has it.

**Content.** The second story-line (the Betreiber's choice pending; recommendation The End of Oil, 40 cards) and
the deck no longer runs dry: a story card may return after 20 weeks if nothing else is there.

**The author.** Every week the log records whether the AI answered and if not, why (error / timeout / rejected
cards with reasons). The author plans ahead: it may return cards for this week and up to ⟦three⟧ weeks ahead,
scheduled; a plan is revised, not replaced, each week.

**What we want to learn:** whether a goal makes the year a story; whether checks make skills and traits felt;
whether the author, once it answers every week, carries the second half.
