Ray flew cargo for twenty-six years – the night runs from the Gulf to Panama, Houston to Bogotá, the ones with
the containers nobody asks about. Then he flew the hurricane season, then he stopped flying and started owning: two
old freighters, a hangar on the coast, a share in a yard where the containers stack to the sky at sunset. He is
tanned the way people are when they stopped caring about it, laughs easily, pays his crews first and himself
last, and has never once told anyone what was in the boxes. He has a daughter in Seoul who calls on Sundays.

If you play Ray, the world comes to you by sea and by air, and you know who is moving what before the news does. A
freighter is a slow way to be in the middle of everything.
